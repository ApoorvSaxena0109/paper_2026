\noindent We submit the enclosed manuscript, ``\textbf{Geopolitical Oil Shocks and Sectoral Heterogeneity: Evidence on Market Transmission and Aggregation Masking},'' for consideration at \textit{Research in International Business and Finance}.

The paper asks whether aggregate market indices accurately capture the economic impact of geopolitical oil shocks. Exploiting five major events---including the recent 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis---we document that sectoral responses are sharply heterogeneous and systematically linked to oil price exposure, yet largely invisible at the index level.

The empirical analysis is extensive. We estimate cross-sectional regressions across six nested specifications with HC3 and GICS-clustered standard errors, validated by double bootstrap inference (2,000 iterations), leave-one-out sensitivity, 72 placebo event studies, and alternative event windows. A multi-event panel with event fixed effects establishes that the oil-beta channel operates consistently across all five events, with event-specific beta estimation confirming robustness to look-ahead concerns. Dynamic horizon analysis reveals that the effect builds persistently from $t+1$ to $t+15$, ruling out short-term noise. Mechanism channels are explored using BEA input-output data on energy intensity, supply chain dependence, and trade exposure.

To formalize the aggregation problem, we introduce a bounded Aggregation Masking Index ($\Psi$) and benchmark it against 72 placebo periods, showing that crisis-period masking meaningfully exceeds normal levels while the underlying sectoral dispersion is twice as large. The analysis extends internationally, linking cross-country equity responses across 14 markets to energy import dependence using World Bank data.

We think the paper fits RIBAF well: it combines a timely geopolitical event with a rigorous multi-event framework, proposes a practical diagnostic for index-level information loss, and speaks directly to international investors and policymakers who rely on aggregate benchmarks during crises.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved this submission.
