We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ``\textbf{Geopolitical Oil Shocks and Sectoral Heterogeneity: Evidence on Market Transmission and Aggregation Masking}'' for consideration for publication in \textit{Research in International Business and Finance}.

This paper addresses a question of direct relevance to international finance practitioners and policymakers: do aggregate market indices accurately reflect the economic impact of geopolitical shocks? Using a multi-event framework spanning five major oil-related crises---including the 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption, the Russia--Ukraine conflict, OPEC+ production cuts, the 2023 Middle East escalation, and the COVID-19 demand collapse---we show that they do not. Sectors with higher oil price sensitivity experience significantly different abnormal returns, yet these heterogeneous responses are largely concealed when aggregated into capitalization-weighted indices.

We believe the paper makes three contributions that align well with the scope and readership of RIBAF.

First, we introduce a bounded Aggregation Masking Index ($\Psi$) that quantifies how much sectoral return variation is netted out through index construction. Benchmarking against 72 non-event placebo dates, we demonstrate that masking is materially stronger during geopolitical crises than during normal market conditions---combined with twice the underlying sectoral dispersion. This finding has practical implications for investors and policymakers who rely on aggregate indices to assess economic impact.

Second, the multi-event panel design moves beyond single-event case studies to establish that the oil-beta transmission channel is a persistent cross-sectional regularity. Consistent with the supply-demand distinction in Kilian (2009), the channel operates asymmetrically across shock types. We further validate these findings through event-specific beta estimation, dynamic horizon analysis, and placebo cross-sectional regressions.

Third, extending the analysis internationally, we show that cross-country variation in equity market responses to the Hormuz crisis is systematically related to energy import dependence---highlighting the relevance of sectoral transmission mechanisms beyond the U.S. market.

The manuscript has not been previously published and is not under consideration at any other journal. All authors have approved the submission.

We appreciate your consideration and look forward to hearing from you.
