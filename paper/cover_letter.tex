\noindent We submit the enclosed manuscript, ``\textbf{Geopolitical Oil Shocks and Sectoral Heterogeneity: Evidence on Market Transmission and Aggregation Masking},'' for consideration at \textit{Research in International Business and Finance}.

The paper asks whether aggregate market indices accurately capture the economic impact of geopolitical oil shocks. Exploiting five major events---including the recent 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis---we document that sectoral responses are sharply heterogeneous and systematically linked to oil price exposure, yet largely invisible at the index level. To formalize this, we introduce a bounded Aggregation Masking Index and benchmark it against placebo periods, showing that crisis-period masking meaningfully exceeds normal levels. The analysis extends internationally, linking cross-country equity responses to energy import dependence.

We think the paper fits RIBAF well: it combines a timely geopolitical event with a multi-event empirical framework, proposes a practical diagnostic for index-level information loss, and speaks directly to international investors and policymakers who rely on aggregate benchmarks during crises.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. We confirm that all authors have approved this submission.
